Solar cars are electric vehicles powered wholly or significantly by direct solar energy, usually via photovoltaic (PV) cells contained in solar panels that convert sunlight directly into electricity. Solar cars combine technology typically used in the aerospace, bicycle, alternative energy and automotive industries. The design of a solar vehicle is severely limited by the amount of energy input into the vehicle, so most solar cars have been built for the purpose of solar car races, which showcase advances in technology.

Most solar cars are built for competitions such as the World Solar Challenge in Australia, a biennial event in which teams from universities and corporations build highly aerodynamic, lightweight vehicles powered exclusively by solar arrays and battery storage. Vehicles typically weigh under 200 kilograms and can travel long distances using only a few kilowatts of solar power, thanks to extreme efficiency measures in aerodynamics, tire rolling resistance, and motor design.

Efforts to bring solar cars to consumer markets have faced steep commercial challenges. Companies such as Lightyear attempted to produce road-legal solar electric vehicles but filed for bankruptcy in 2023 after struggling to scale production and manage costs, before restructuring around a smaller model. Aptera Motors has faced repeated production delays for its three-wheeled solar electric vehicle, first announced years before any units reached customers. The fundamental challenge is that a car's surface area limits how much solar power it can realistically capture compared to the energy needed for daily driving.

The concept dates back to 1955, when General Motors displayed the Sunmobile, a small model car powered by a selenium photovoltaic cell, at a Chicago exhibition. Since then, solar cell efficiency has improved dramatically. Modern solar arrays using crystalline silicon or gallium arsenide cells can achieve efficiency rates of 15-25%, with some experimental multi-junction cells exceeding 30%, allowing arrays to produce over 2 kilowatts of power under ideal sunlight conditions.
